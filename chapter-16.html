Vision 20 Compared With AREDS2

Why formulas that share carotenoids are not necessarily interchangeable or appropriate for the same people.

AREDS2 has a defined evidence base

The National Eye Institute studied the AREDS and AREDS2 formulations in people at risk for progression to advanced age-related macular degeneration. The AREDS2 formula includes vitamin C, vitamin E, zinc, copper, lutein, and zeaxanthin in specified amounts. It is used for particular stages of AMD—not as a universal vision supplement.

NEI guidance says AREDS2 supplements may help people with intermediate AMD or certain people with late AMD in one eye. They do not prevent AMD in people who do not have it and are not useful for every stage. An eye-care professional should determine whether the formula is appropriate.

How Vision 20 differs

Vision 20 label records describe a different mix. They include zinc and copper, carotenoids, and botanical extracts such as bilberry, ginkgo, and maritime pine bark. They do not match the full AREDS2 ingredient list and amounts. Similarity in the presence of lutein, zeaxanthin, zinc, or copper does not make the products equivalent.

The zinc amount listed in Vision 20 public label records is 50 mg per capsule. The adult upper intake level from the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements is 40 mg per day from all sources. The amount used in a specific eye-disease formula is not a reason to self-select a different high-zinc product.

Do not switch without your eye-care professional

If your eye doctor recommended an AREDS2 product, ask before changing to Vision 20. If you have no AMD diagnosis, do not start AREDS2 or Vision 20 merely because an advertisement suggests that everyone needs an eye supplement. Get advice based on your examination, health history, medications, and diet.
