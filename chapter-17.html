The Eye-Care Routine That Supplements Cannot Replace

Practical habits and regular examinations that remain central to protecting vision.

Make eye examinations routine

Many eye diseases develop without obvious early symptoms. The National Eye Institute recommends asking an eye-care professional how often a dilated eye examination is appropriate for your age and risks. Its guidance notes that adults over 60 and people with certain risk factors may need examinations every one to two years, while people with diabetes or high blood pressure often need at least annual exams. Individual needs vary.

Bring an up-to-date medication and supplement list to appointments. Tell the clinician about changes in vision, family history, diabetes, high blood pressure, previous eye surgery, and any sudden symptoms. Keep recommended follow-up appointments even when vision seems stable.

Use sensible daily protection

Wear sunglasses that block ultraviolet rays when outdoors, use appropriate protective eyewear for work and sports, avoid smoking, and maintain general health. Take breaks during prolonged close-up screen work and adjust lighting to reduce discomfort. These habits do not guarantee that eye disease will be prevented, but they support a sensible approach to eye health.

Know when not to wait

Sudden flashes, many new floaters, a shadow or curtain in the field of vision, or sudden vision loss need urgent medical attention. Do not postpone care while waiting to see whether a supplement helps.

Vision 20 may be considered only as a supplement decision after reviewing its ingredients and risks. It should not replace glasses, prescribed eye drops, injections, surgery, diabetes care, blood pressure management, or any other treatment recommended by a clinician.
